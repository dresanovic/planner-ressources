## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Choose and identify the teaching algorithm (Priority: P1)

The operator selects Standard, Cohort or Lecturer through installation policy; the planner uses the existing workflow and sees which algorithm is running.

**Why this priority**: One effective selector must control every supported teaching-generation path.

**Independent Test**: Run one-course and twenty-course isolated selections under each policy value using normal local startup and an explicit deployment path.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** each valid selector in turn, **When** preparation and Start occur, **Then** the named real algorithm executes and its identity appears read-only throughout preparation, progress and review/results.
2. **Given** a valid legacy policy without a selector, **When** prepared, **Then** Standard is selected and existing budget values remain effective.
3. **Given** an invalid explicit selector, malformed policy or invalid budgets, **When** new preparation/Start is attempted, **Then** it is blocked with operator guidance and zero saved-plan changes.
4. **Given** material policy changes after preparation, **When** a new Start is attempted, **Then** fresh preparation is required.
5. **Given** an already-started operation, **When** policy changes, **Then** execution, review and acceptance retain its captured settings; repeated Start returns that operation, while a fresh retry reads current policy.
6. **Given** a normal local run without an explicit override, **When** preparation reads policy, **Then** the logical repository policy is read independently of process working directory; an explicitly configured deployment path takes precedence.
7. **Given** a missing or unreadable resolved policy file, **When** new preparation/Start is attempted, **Then** it is blocked with actionable German guidance identifying the policy location and asking the operator to provision/correct it; no default algorithm runs, saved work is unchanged, and unrelated startup/health functions remain available.

### User Story 2 - Retain Standard behavior (Priority: P1)

The planner can use the current algorithm under the Standard name without changing its scheduling priorities or evidence.

**Why this priority**: Existing results provide the comparison baseline for the alternatives.

**Independent Test**: Compare completed deterministic results with recorded current outputs on identical isolated snapshots.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** unchanged baseline inputs and enough time to finish, **When** Standard runs, **Then** sessions, assigned resources, coverage, objective ordering and proof classifications match current behavior.
2. **Given** bounded search, **When** Standard ends, **Then** valid-checkpoint, proof/bound and acceptance protections remain; identical deadline race timing is not required.
3. **Given** any teaching selector, **When** exam generation runs, **Then** the exam algorithm and its behavior remain unchanged.

### User Story 3 - Give a cohort a steady timetable while courses finish (Priority: P1)

Cohort plans selected courses by cohort. A course normally follows a steady weekly teaching rhythm until finished, while courses together provide an evenly distributed cohort timetable across the semester. An individual course need not run throughout the semester. Weeks with extra capacity can carry increased teaching.

**Why this priority**: This is the user-confirmed cohort behavior, rather than simply allocating all courses proportionally across the semester.

**Independent Test**: Use a cohort with several courses, ordinary weeks, an intensive week, fixed teaching and a 22-LE course with preferred size 3 LE and permitted sizes 2–4 LE.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** that 22-LE course, seven consecutive feasible ordinary weeks and no competing higher-priority intensive opportunity, **When** Cohort plans it, **Then** it has seven weekly sessions: six of 3 LE and one of 4 LE, totaling exactly 22 LE; the 4-LE session may occur in any of the seven weeks.
2. **Given** enough semester weeks to stretch those sessions further, **When** Cohort compares alternatives, **Then** making the individual course span the entire semester is not a scheduling goal; its normal rhythm continues until completion.
3. **Given** multiple courses whose durations differ, **When** Cohort plans the cohort, **Then** it seeks steady aggregate weekly teaching across the semester, allowing courses to start and finish at different times rather than requiring every course to be active throughout.
4. **Given** an intensive week with more usable teaching opportunities and a course normally taught on one day per week, **When** Cohort distributes teaching, **Then** that course may be taught on two separate days that week, keeping preferred session sizes where feasible, and other courses may also be introduced; required-LE coverage and all hard rules remain protected.
5. **Given** shared resources across cohorts and scarce course opportunities, **When** placement and bounded repair occur, **Then** provisional assignments can be revisited across groups but protected bookings remain fixed; unsuccessful repair restores occupancy and remaining demand exactly.
6. **Given** a preferred session split with an otherwise avoidable remainder, **When** permitted session-size redistribution can complete coverage, **Then** the search considers that redistribution instead of silently stranding LE.

### User Story 4 - Balance a lecturer across courses and cohorts (Priority: P1)

Lecturer is a distinct algorithm that groups by lecturer across selected courses/cohorts and prioritizes a steady weekly rhythm for each course until completion, even when the lecturer's total weekly LE varies. Overall lecturer load balance is secondary to that course rhythm. Compact days are not its primary goal.

For each course it divides required LE among all active eligible lecturers in fixed lecturer-code order, gives rounding extras to the earlier lecturers, and schedules their portions sequentially. This is a hard allocation/sequence rule for Lecturer, not a soft workload preference. Cohort and Standard retain existing lecturer-eligibility behavior.

**Why this priority**: A different grouping owner supplies a different planning approach, rather than a relabelled Cohort algorithm.

**Independent Test**: Use a lecturer shared across cohorts, fixed teaching, multiple courses and a course with alternative eligible lecturers; compare the lecturer-oriented distribution and shared course demand.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** selected courses across cohorts, **When** Lecturer runs, **Then** lecturer grouping and each course's steady weekly rhythm guide search; relevant fixed teaching contributes to lecturer load and occupancy.
2. **Given** a fixture with competing cohort/lecturer preferences, **When** both algorithms run separately, **Then** each follows its own grouping and balance rule; matching labels alone do not establish distinct implementations.
3. **Given** one or more eligible lecturers for one course and no existing explicit portions, **When** Lecturer plans it, **Then** it divides course LE equally among all of them and schedules their portions in successive lecturer blocks, with no alternation. With two lecturers, the first lecturer's sessions all precede the second's; with more lecturers, the same rule applies to each consecutive pair. Every placed LE is deducted exactly once from both its portion and the shared course demand.
4. **Given** confirmed lecturer portions from an available allocation feature, **When** assignments are made, **Then** those portions remain nontransferable hard boundaries and take precedence over derived equal shares. Without explicit portions, Lecturer derives the sequential shares required by FR-025; this adds no allocation-administration UI.
5. **Given** otherwise equal valid alternatives with equal common higher-priority scores, one preserving each course's ordinary weekly cadence and another changing that cadence to flatten the lecturer's total weekly LE, **When** Lecturer chooses, **Then** it prefers the course-cadence alternative even if the lecturer's weekly total varies.
6. **Given** an intensive week with extra feasible opportunities, **When** Lecturer plans a normally once-weekly course, **Then** it may use two distinct teaching days for that course and also introduce other courses, retaining preferred session sizes where feasible and respecting remaining demand and all hard rules.
7. **Given** lecturer codes B and A provided in either input order, **When** Lecturer derives portions, **Then** A precedes B, with stable identity breaking any equal-code tie; availability or potential coverage cannot reverse this sequence.
8. **Given** A cannot complete its nonzero portion but B is available, **When** search stops, **Then** any valid partial A block may be reviewed, B's block remains unstarted, and remaining-LE feedback explains the predecessor blockage without claiming global infeasibility or transferring LE.

### User Story 5 - Understand and accept a safe result (Priority: P1)

The planner reviews coverage, remaining LE and truthful search evidence, then accepts or discards the entire selection using existing controls.

**Why this priority**: Heuristic usefulness must preserve validity, saved work and conscious acceptance.

**Independent Test**: Exercise early heuristic completion, partial/complete unproven candidates, repair-limit, deadline, zero placement, cancellation, failure and stale-input cases; compare saved state around each decision.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** early hard-valid heuristic completion with all LE placed but preferences unproven, **When** reviewed before timeout, **Then** no automatic save occurs and explicit whole-selection acceptance is available.
2. **Given** partial coverage including a selected course with zero placed LE, **When** reviewed, **Then** every selected course is shown with reconciled totals, reasons or uncertainty, and selected/manual replacement consequences including any emptied course.
3. **Given** zero placed LE across the selection, **When** search ends, **Then** no usable candidate/save action is offered and saved work is unchanged.
4. **Given** explicit acceptance, fresh inputs and independently validated assignments, **When** save occurs, **Then** all selected changes commit atomically and protected bookings/publications remain unchanged.
5. **Given** invalid/stale input, cancelled/dismissed review or pre-commit failure, **When** the operation ends, **Then** no candidate changes are saved and late results cannot revive discarded review.
6. **Given** changed policy after Start but fresh planning inputs, **When** acceptance occurs, **Then** policy change alone does not reject or recompute the captured candidate.
7. **Given** a search miss or full coverage without preference proof, **When** results are displayed, **Then** termination, coverage and proof are separate; no invented bound or infeasibility claim is shown.

### Edge Cases

- Omitted selector defaults to Standard; explicit null, empty, misspelled, wrong-type or unknown selectors do not.
- Duplicate/unknown policy keys, malformed files and atomic file replacement retain existing strict validation; formatting-only edits do not change effective policy evidence.
- Normal local path resolution must work from a different working directory; deployment migration must address missing/unreadable files.
- Partial weeks, holidays, unavailable days, no-capacity weeks and competing occupancy can interrupt an otherwise steady rhythm; balance never overrides legality.
- One ordinary-week teaching day and two intensive-week teaching days per course are preferences, not hard weekly limits: extra distinct days remain legal when they improve required-LE coverage. The one-session-per-course/date rule remains hard.
- Overlapping weekly/dated opportunities and shared time must not double-count capacity, teaching or intensive overlap.
- A larger intensive-week load must not be confused with requiring every course to occupy every semester week.
- A 22-LE course must not lose its final LE through an inflexible 3-LE split, nor allocate 24 LE; valid sizes remain mandatory.
- Multiple eligible lecturers share remaining demand, including across repair and rollback; confirmed allocations cannot be transferred.
- Equal sequential lecturer portions must account for nondivisible LE totals, portions smaller than the minimum session size, portion remainders incompatible with session sizes, and an unavailable predecessor lecturer. All eligible lecturers participate; shares must not be silently transferred or lecturer order interleaved to improve coverage.
- Deadline/cancellation can occur during enumeration, checks, scoring, repair or finalization; no phase may run unbounded.
- Complete coverage is not proof of optimal intensive use, balance, variety or continuity; sufficient aggregate capacity is not proof of a feasible timetable.
- Policy changes after Start do not invalidate otherwise fresh acceptance; relevant planning-input changes do.
- A committed save followed by a lost response must reconcile as saved rather than report confirmed cancellation or duplicate the save.

#### Heuristic preference and week definitions

These explicit defaults make FR-012–019 independently testable. They apply to Cohort and Lecturer only; Standard retains its current score order.

1. **Calendar weeks and opportunities**: Use institution-local Monday–Sunday weeks, clipped to each course's effective period. Count a date once after merging overlapping permitted windows and applying known holidays, exclusions, resource availability, eligibility, capacity and fixed occupancy. A course's eligible week has at least one hard-valid minimum-size candidate before competition with other selected tentative work. A cohort/lecturer's scoring weeks are the union of relevant selected-course eligible weeks and weeks with that owner's protected fixed teaching inside the selection's overall planning span. Exams block occupancy but are not teaching load.
2. **Instruction and load**: Weekly load is instructional LE, including relevant protected fixed teaching once. Do not include breaks or exam minutes as teaching LE. Alternative eligible lecturers do not duplicate the course's LE or any placed session's load. Overlapping intensive windows count actual instructional overlap once, in the same units as the existing intensive objective, excluding breaks.
3. **Preferred-size deviation**: After the three common higher priorities, minimize the sum of absolute deviations of generated session sizes from each course's preferred LE size. Use its existing midpoint default when no preference is set. Thus six 3-LE sessions plus one 4-LE session has deviation 1 for the 22-LE example; six 3-LE plus two 2-LE sessions has deviation 2 and is worse when higher priorities tie. This is not a fixed split: search may redistribute sizes to improve coverage or another higher priority.
4. **Course rhythm**: Next minimize skipped eligible weekly teaching opportunities strictly between a course's first and last generated session, plus excess sessions above the week's soft cadence. Ordinary-week cadence is one session; in a week with a feasible marked intensive opportunity it is up to two sessions on separate dates. An intervening intensive week counts up to two missed opportunities, limited by the number of distinct feasible dates. First/last active weeks incur no missing-opportunity penalty: a course may start later, finish earlier, or need only one session in its final intensive week. Zero-opportunity weeks do not incur a gap penalty. Extra sessions above cadence are soft penalties, never new hard prohibitions; coverage and intensive priorities may justify them. Session-size preference and this rhythm take precedence over flattening total lecturer load.
5. **Owner balance**: Next minimize uneven aggregate weekly instructional load for the owner: cohort for Cohort, lecturer for Lecturer. For each owner, sum `weekly LE squared / week weight` across its scoring weeks. An ordinary scoring week has weight 1; a week containing a feasible marked intensive opportunity for that owner has weight 2. Compare the sum over owners. With the same assigned total, this favors equal LE in ordinary weeks and permits approximately twice that load in intensive weeks. Do not normalize ordinary-week load by raw available minutes; a partial boundary week retains weight 1 if it has a feasible date, while a zero-opportunity/no-fixed-load week is excluded. Availability always limits actual placements. This is a soft balancing preference, not a required weekly quota or a promise that increased capacity will be filled.
6. **Room continuity**: Next minimize extra distinct rooms beyond one per cohort/date, then room changes between consecutive generated sessions of each course. Relevant fixed teaching contributes to the cohort/date distinct-room set; do not infer a permanent room or change protected assignments.
7. **Stable traversal and ties**: Prioritize outstanding courses with fewer distinct feasible date/start/size opportunities, excluding duplicate source windows and resource alternatives, then fewer eligible dates, then stable course identity. Cohort traverses cohort groups; Lecturer traverses lecturer groups, rechecking shared course demand, the lecturer's remaining portion and its predecessor's completion before each assignment. Traversal MUST NOT bypass sequential portion order. Group scarcity is the smallest outstanding-course opportunity count in that group, then stable group identity. Tie placements by date, start time, course identity, session size, normalized lecturer code/identity and room code/identity. Compare equally scored final alternatives by their sorted assignment sequences using that order. Equal completed search work on identical snapshots must be deterministic; deadline races are excluded.

For both strategies the ordered comparison is therefore: coverage, intensive teaching, intensive variety, preferred-size deviation, course-rhythm penalty, owner-balance penalty, extra cohort/day rooms, course room changes, stable assignment order. No weighted sum may trade away a higher component for a lower one. Different owner grouping, fixed-load inputs and owner-balance evaluation make Cohort and Lecturer distinct searches even though their course-rhythm and intensive-week rules coincide.

#### Concrete preference fixtures

- **Course rhythm**: With ten feasible ordinary weeks and the 22-LE course alone, seven consecutive weekly sessions have zero skipped-week penalty; spreading seven sessions across all ten weeks has three skipped eligible weeks and is worse. A 4-LE session is allowed at any position; stable ties select a reproducible position without a special “last-session remainder” rule.
- **Coverage before weekly frequency**: For each heuristic, use a 6-LE course, preferred size 3 and allowed sizes 2–4, with two feasible dates in its only ordinary planning week. Two 3-LE sessions schedule all 6 LE and outrank any one-day plan, which places at most 4 LE. Separately, a 9-LE course with the same sizes and three feasible dates in its only intensive week may use three 3-LE sessions rather than leave at least 1 LE open under a two-day cap. No session shares a course/date and all hard rules hold. These fixtures test permission and coverage priority, not a new guarantee that bounded search always finds full coverage.
- **Cohort semester distribution**: With two 6-LE courses, preference 3, four ordinary weeks and one cohort, assign one course in weeks 1–2 and the other in weeks 3–4. The cohort load is `[3,3,3,3]`, balance cost 36, preferred-size deviation 0 and course-gap penalty 0. Overlapping both in weeks 1–2 gives `[6,6,0,0]`, cost 72 and otherwise equal lower measures, so the sequential arrangement is preferred when higher priorities tie. Session times/resource choices in the fixture allow both alternatives without hard conflicts.
- **Intensive balance**: For three scoring weeks with weights `[1,2,1]` and 12 assigned LE, loads `[3,6,3]` have balance cost 36, compared with cost 40.5 for `[6,3,3]`. Compare the balance component only when higher components tie; intensive-priority evidence must remain separate from this score. Two distinct dates may carry the same course's sessions in the intensive week while another course is introduced.
- **Lecturer rhythm before load**: With two 6-LE courses at preference 3 and four ordinary weeks, compare sessions in weeks 1–2 for each course (lecturer load `[6,6,0,0]`, course-gap penalty 0) against weeks 1 and 3 for the first and 2 and 4 for the second (load `[3,3,3,3]`, course-gap penalty 2). With common higher priorities tied, Lecturer prefers the first despite its less-even aggregate lecturer load. This pair tests rhythm priority, not a claim that no third arrangement is better.
- **Distinct owner**: For one 3-LE session eligible in either of two ordinary weeks, provide the selected cohort with 3 fixed LE in week 1 taught by another lecturer, and the eligible lecturer with 3 fixed LE in week 2 for another cohort. Use nonoverlapping dates/times. Cohort prefers week 2 (its load `[3,3]`, cost 18 versus 36); Lecturer prefers week 1 (its load `[3,3]`, cost 18 versus 36). Higher components and room preferences tie. This demonstrates actual grouping-led behavior on the same snapshot.
- **Sequential lecturers**: With a 22-LE course, two eligible lecturers and no explicit allocations, derive 11 LE each. Under feasible dates/session sizes, every first-lecturer session precedes every second-lecturer session. A candidate alternating lecturers or assigning 12/10 is invalid even if its aggregate load score improves. Failed repair restores both portion balances, predecessor state and shared remaining LE.
- **More than two lecturers**: With 24 LE and three eligible lecturers, derive 8 LE each and schedule three successive lecturer blocks. Under feasible dates/session sizes, every first-lecturer session precedes every second-lecturer session, which in turn precedes every third-lecturer session. No lecturer is silently omitted and no block resumes after its successor starts.
- **Rounded shares**: With 23 LE and two lecturers, derive 12/11 in lecturer-sequence order; with 22 LE and three, derive 8/7/7. Verify that shares sum to the original requirement, the first lecturers receive the whole-LE remainder, and assignments/rollback preserve each share. Zero shares generate no sessions and need no chronological block; an unrepresentable nonzero share remains open and cannot be transferred or bypassed to complete a successor.
- **Fixed sequence**: Supply eligible lecturer codes B then A and reverse the input list on a repeat run. For 23 LE, both runs derive A:12 then B:11, independently of availability. If A can place only 9 of its 12 LE while B has free dates, the review shows A scheduled 9/remaining 3 and B scheduled 0/remaining 11, with B blocked by A's incomplete portion. A wholly unavailable A yields no usable course sessions; other selected courses may still yield a usable selection-level partial result. Injected B-before-A, interleaved, wrong-code-order and wrong-share candidates must be rejected.
- **Bounds and interruptions**: Exercise the 1000th versus attempted 1001st repair alternative, the eight-session displacement boundary, cancellation/deadline during enumeration and repair, and the five-second checkpoint cadence. No accepted result can contain an incomplete rollback or depend on mutable provisional state. For early termination, the displayed reason identifies whether permitted exploration completed, a repair bound was reached, the deadline expired or cancellation was registered.

## Clarifications

### Session 2026-11-09

- Cohort and Lecturer are distinct algorithms; clarification questions are presented individually, one algorithm at a time.
- Cohort ordinary-week example: 22 LE with preference 3 and permitted sizes 2–4 means seven weekly sessions, six of 3 and one of 4, with the 4-LE session allowed anywhere. Overall cohort teaching should be evenly distributed, but an individual course need not span the semester.
- Cohort extra-capacity weeks: mixed increase, including other courses and allowing the same normally once-weekly course to teach on two separate days in an intensive week.
- Lecturer weekly balance: user selected B — preserve each course's steady weekly rhythm even when the lecturer's total weekly LE varies. This takes precedence over aggregate lecturer-load smoothing, below common coverage/intensive priorities.
- Lecturer intensive weeks: user selected A — allow two distinct teaching days for a normally once-weekly course, alongside additional courses, matching the confirmed mixed increase for Cohort.
- Missing policy file: user selected A — block new planning runs and explain how the operator can fix the configuration; do not silently use Standard/default time limits.
- Q: Should the new Cohort and Lecturer algorithms be allowed to exceed one teaching day per course in ordinary weeks or two in intensive weeks when that schedules more required LE? → A: A — prefer one ordinary-week day and two intensive-week days, but allow additional distinct teaching days when they increase scheduled required LE; all hard rules and common higher priorities remain unchanged.
- Q: For the Lecturer algorithm, should a course normally keep the same lecturer across its sessions when several lecturers are eligible? → A: Divide the course LE equally between lecturers and schedule the first lecturer's portion before the second's, sequentially rather than alternating. This replaces the proposed continuity-versus-free-alternation choices.
- Q: If a course has three or more eligible lecturers, should Lecturer divide its LE equally among all of them in sequential blocks? → A: A — divide LE among all eligible lecturers and schedule their portions sequentially.
- Q: When LE cannot be divided evenly, which lecturers should receive the extra whole LE? → A: A — give one extra whole LE to each earlier lecturer in sequence until the remainder is exhausted: 23 LE across two becomes 12/11; 22 across three becomes 8/7/7.
- Q: How should Lecturer decide which lecturer teaches first, second, and so on? → A: B — always use ascending lecturer-code order, with stable lecturer identity breaking ties; do not reorder for availability or additional coverage. This is the user's choice of the simplest predictable ordering.
